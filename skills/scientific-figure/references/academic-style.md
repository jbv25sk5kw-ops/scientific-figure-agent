# Academic Style

## Usage modes

- UM01 Manuscript — minimal text, no slogan box, high whitespace, restrained palette, editable labels
- UM02 Thesis — slightly more explanatory, still publication-oriented
- UM03 Presentation — stronger hierarchy and larger text
- UM04 Competition — more narrative impact without sacrificing accuracy

## Academic pale palette

Default manuscript/thesis style:
- near-white background
- low saturation
- medium-high lightness
- 3–5 dominant hue families
- dark gray text
- dark color reserved for critical arrows/outlines

Semantic families:
- healthy/normal: pale desaturated blue or blue-gray
- pathology/damage: muted dusty rose / pale terracotta
- immune components: soft lavender / muted indigo
- protection/therapy: pale teal / muted blue-green
- neutral structure: warm/cool gray

Color must not be the only carrier of meaning. Critical states should also be distinguishable by text, shape, border, nucleotide identity, or another redundant visual cue.

## Visual anchor budget

For manuscript/thesis:
- one primary anchor
- up to two secondary anchors
- all other elements subordinate

If more than three regions compete equally, simplify or merge.

## Text budget

Manuscript:
- title preferably outside figure
- 2–6 primary labels per major region
- avoid explanatory sentences and slogans

Thesis:
- concise title allowed
- 3–8 primary labels per major region
- short explanatory phrases only when useful

If text occupies roughly >20–30% of the canvas, simplify.

## Visual grammar

- confirmed flow/process: clean solid arrow
- inhibition/blockade: T-shaped blunt line
- hypothesis: dashed arrow
- indirect relation: dotted line or dotted arrow
- label annotation: leader line without arrowhead when possible
- workflow progression: flat, simple arrows with consistent width and arrowhead size
- model/data input: one consistent connector style distinct from biological activation when ambiguity is possible
- delivery/intervention: label with `delivery`, `transduction`, `administration`, `injection`, or a more specific term when needed
- avoid crossed leader lines
- avoid thick cartoon outlines
- avoid heavy shadows, glow, bevels, and decorative gradients on connectors
- use very light panel tint only when grouping adds meaning

For manuscript/thesis figures, large green check marks, badge-like success icons, and promotional symbols should generally be replaced by concise labels such as `selected`, `prioritized`, `validated`, or `corrected`.

Avoid relying on red-versus-green alone for mutant/pathologic versus corrected/normal states. Pair color with explicit labels or sequence-level notation such as `A→G`, `mutant`, or `corrected`.

Avoid decorative ellipses when they make omitted content ambiguous. Prefer grouped labels such as `multiple candidates` or `additional designs`.

For detailed connector rules, use `arrow-symbol-semantics.md`.

## Typography

Prefer Arial/Helvetica/Inter/Source Sans or equivalent clean sans serif. Use dark gray/black body labels, consistent hierarchy, and editable text when possible.
